The Harbour H

An H for Home, Homer and Harbour: its crossbar is a hull and a sail rises in the counter above it. The ship, safe home. Below the hull, the counter is a doorway.

The mark

Built from a few round numbers

The H is 24 × 24 modules of 8 units. Each stem is 6 modules wide with continuous-curvature corners (radius 14, 60 % smoothing, the kind Apple uses: as round to the eye as round 1’s, without a kink where the curve starts). The deck, the H’s crossbar, sits at 13 modules; the hull is 2 modules deep at the stems and its underside is one circle of radius 10 modules, so the keel lands on 17 (a 3–4–5 triangle: 48, 64, 80). The sail stands on 11 modules, its mast side at 10, its clew at 16; its back edge is one arc bellied 23°, every corner radius 3. The sail keeps 2 modules from the deck and from the stem, so it stays a separate shape down to 24 px.

Refined from round 1: equal gaps around the sail (the right one was 11 units and closed up at 32 px), the sail rebuilt as one arc, smooth corners, the hull on a clean circle, everything on the module grid. Its visual centre sits 1 % above the geometric centre, where the eye expects it.

Small sizes

Below 48 px the mark has two hand-fitted cuts. Use the file for the size you draw; never scale the 16 px cut up.

Versions

Symbol, two lockups, four looks

The horizontal lockup is the default. Its wordmark stands on the keel line with capitals 11 modules tall, one stem (6 modules) from the symbol. The stacked lockup sets the wordmark 7 modules tall, 5 modules under the symbol. The wordmark never appears without the symbol.

Clear space and minimum sizes

One stem of air

Keep a clear space of one stem width, a quarter of the symbol’s height, on every side of the symbol or a lockup. It scales with the logo. Nothing else enters it: no text, no edges, no other logos.

Colour

One teal, light and dark, on Apple’s neutrals

One accent colour carries the brand; everything else is Apple’s system greys, as on classic’s home page. The accent has a light-mode value for light backgrounds and a lighter dark-mode value for dark ones. Two teals are on the table: classic’s, and one turned toward blue (“teal blue”). Same lightness and chroma, so contrast is the same; only the hue differs.

Recommendation: Classic teal. Self-hosting is a blue category (Home Assistant, Nextcloud, Portainer, TrueNAS, CasaOS); the green-leaning teal stands apart from all of them, and on a phone it doesn’t join the crowd of blue icons. It is the colour the family already knows from classic’s home page, it reads warmer next to the brief’s “calm, warm”, and it has close Pantone matches (7719 C, ΔE00 1.7; 7472 C, 2.2) where the blue teal’s light value has none closer than 3.8. Pick the blue teal if “teal blue” meant bluer: it passes every check too. To switch the kit: python3 source/build.py --teal blue, then tests.py --teal blue and guide.py in the same folder.

Rules

  • On the light-mode teal, use white (6.1:1). On the dark-mode teal, use black (8.5:1); white on it is only 2.5:1.
  • The light-mode teal is for light backgrounds, the dark-mode teal for dark ones. Swapped, they fall to 3.5:1 and 2.5:1.
  • Accent as text or icons: both teals pass AA (4.5:1) on Ground and Card in their own mode.
  • Greyscale and one-colour versions carry the whole mark; no meaning sits in colour alone.

CMYK: converted to U.S. Web Coated (SWOP), relative colorimetric; greys as black only. Ask the printer for theirs (in Europe usually PSO Coated v3). Pantone: the nearest PANTONE Solid Coated by CIEDE2000 against Pantone’s published sRGB values; approximate, so check a physical guide before printing. All values: palette/palette.json, CSS: palette/tokens.css.

Type

Figtree for the name, the system font for everything else

The product speaks in the system font (SF on Apple devices, Inter elsewhere, as in classic). SF’s licence doesn’t allow it in a logo, so the wordmark is set in Figtree SemiBold (SIL Open Font License, logo use allowed) and converted to outlines: no font is needed to show it.

Why Figtree: its proportions and low stroke contrast sit easily next to SF, so the name and the interface look related; its round o, e and a echo the hull’s arc and soften the heavy symbol, which suits “calm, warm”; and it is different enough from SF and Inter that the name reads as a logo, not as a label. Inter was the other candidate; it is what the UI already shows on Linux and Windows, so the name would look like plain text there.

Custom: tracking −10/1000 em, H–o closed by 20 and s–L by 15, every outer corner rounded at a quarter of the stem width, as round as the symbol’s corners look. On dark backgrounds the wordmark is weight 580 instead of 600, about 3 % thinner. Figtree also sets brand moments such as the social card; never retype the wordmark, use the files.

Icons

Favicon, phone, desktop

Tested on browser tabs (light and dark Chrome, Firefox, Safari colours, 1× and 2×): the SVG favicon holds 4.1–4.9:1 on dark tabs and 4.6–6.1:1 on light ones. Browsers that read only the ICO show the light-mode teal on dark tabs at about 2:1: there, the shape carries it. See tests/favicon-tabs-1x.png and -2x.png.

Do and don’t

Do

  • Use the light files on light backgrounds and the dark files on dark ones.
  • Use the symbol alone where the name is already present: app icons, tabs, avatars, the app’s own header next to its title.
  • Give it its clear space, and the size file that matches the size you draw (16, 24, 32, 48 px).
  • On photos, use the white version on a calm area, or the app-icon tile.
  • In the UI, inline the master’s path with fill="currentColor" and let the tokens colour it.

Don’t

Each of these breaks the mark. Use the files as they are.
